I have watched all three and read the coverage analysis. My overall reaction is that the engineering is excellent and the argument is incomplete. Thirteen of the twenty-two things we said this material had to convey land properly, six land only partly, and three are absent. That is a respectable result for a first cut and an unacceptable one for anything shown to a customer or to the board, because two of the three absences are the two that a commercial audience cares about most.

Let me start with what works, because it genuinely does.

The build-side story is the strongest thing in the sequence, and it is the part I expected to be weakest. "The agent does the typing. The schema catches the mistakes. The human owns the decision. And if it is wrong, it is a git revert." That is four sentences and it settles the question every board member was going to ask me about AI touching our production data. I could repeat that line in a governance committee without preparing for it. The same is true of the address-correction route — a mangled address in, a confidence score out, above the threshold it proceeds, below it a steward sees it, and the model can run inside our own infrastructure. That last clause matters more than the narration seems to realise; for a business holding customer addresses in eleven countries it is frequently the whole conversation.

I also want to record that the framing sentence — "the build side produces trust, and the consumption side spends it" — is the best line in the material. It is the sentence that turns four separate videos into one argument. Whoever wrote it should be told.

Now the gaps that matter to me.

**Nobody makes a decision.** This is my first and largest objection. Across three videos I am shown a question answered, an alert raised, and a card posted to an operations channel, and in none of those cases does a named person do anything. The Singapore answer is a good example of the problem: it correctly explains why our delivery promise accuracy fell to sixty-nine percent, cites the NinjaVan amendment, and stops. But the reason that failure cost us money is that fourteen thousand customers a month were given a promise we had already stopped honouring, for three weeks, while the website said otherwise. The interesting moment is not the explanation. It is Sophie seeing it on day two instead of week three and changing the published promise that afternoon. We show the diagnosis and omit the treatment.

I asked the original question behind this programme — why can I not see what is happening in my own supply chain. Seeing it was never the objective. Acting sooner was.

**No number moves.** The sequence opens on a hundred and twenty-seven million dollars a year and closes on the same hundred and twenty-seven million. Inventory record accuracy is seventy-four percent against a target of ninety-seven. Not one of those figures is shown improving, and no recovered amount is claimed anywhere. We have therefore produced twelve minutes of material that demonstrates capability and asserts no value. I would rather we claimed something defensible and modest — one measure, honestly sourced — than nothing at all. Pick the one we can evidence from the tenant and put it in.

**The preview labelling is not good enough, and this one is a compliance matter as much as a marketing one.** The narration says "everything you're about to see is running on a live tenant" and "it exposes the real tenant, not a simulation." Both are true and both are, in context, misleading: declarative pipelines and the MCP server are preview capabilities. The only place we admit that is small print on a single slide, which no listener will register. The three conversational agents are handled correctly — calling them Qlik Answers capabilities rather than products is exactly right and shows the discipline exists. Apply the same discipline to the other two. I would rather under-claim and be believed. If Elena's team reviews this and finds we told a customer a preview capability was generally available, that is a conversation I do not want to have.

Three further things I want addressed, in descending order of how much they bother me.

Pipelines as code is presented as the alternative to clicking, which is a false choice and it undersells us. The point is that the same file is editable in the interface, in a developer's editor, or by an agent — one definition, one schema, one history. As narrated, an engineer watching this reasonably concludes we are asking them to give up the visual editor. Say the three paths explicitly.

The three ways of consuming data are never shown reading the same thing. Sheets, answers and predictions each appear over a different source, and the phrase "the same two gold data products" is used only about the agents. That undoes some of the governance argument the second video works hard to build.

And on the consumption side there is no human gate at all. A sentence of plain English becomes a governed object in a governed app, and nobody says who approved it. We are careful about this everywhere else — the glossary terms wait for approval, the low-confidence address goes to a steward. The one place a person authors something that the business will then read as governed, we are silent. Name the approver.

What I want before this leaves the building:

1. One worked decision in each of the analytics and outcomes videos. A named role, what they see, what they do, and what changes. Use the Singapore promise, and use Sophie.
2. One quantified result we can evidence, stated plainly, with its source.
3. Preview capabilities labelled in the narration, not only on screen. If it is preview, the voice says preview.
4. The three authoring paths stated in the integrations video.
5. The consumption-side approval step named.

Points one, two and three are conditions, not suggestions. I am content for four and five to be handled in the next revision if the schedule is tight.

For the avoidance of doubt: I am pleased with this. The technical argument is sound, the honesty about the agents is exactly the register I want us in, and the build-side material is better than anything we have produced on this programme. It needs the business half of the story added to it.
